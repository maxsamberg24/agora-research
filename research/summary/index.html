More ways to connect than ever. Less connection.

Twenty conversations on how young adults connect socially — and why the bar keeps winning.

Picture kids at recess where everyone is crowded onto the playground. It isn't much fun, but it's reliable so nobody leaves. Around the edges people call out the games they'd rather be playing — flag football, jump rope, dance off — but nobody moves, because the fun was never in the game. It's in the density.

The playground is the bar.
We didn't choose it.

0/20 Not one person made a friend at a bar.Every setting they named was one they went back to.
  • New people, yes. Strangers, no. Every path to meeting someone new ran through someone they already knew.
  • Friend groups don't merge. The problem isn't that people are alone. It's that the groups never touch.
  • Discovery is not the bottleneck. People are flooded with options, not starved. Another events feed solves a problem nobody has.
  • Technology = good at logistics, bad at people. "It's easy to find things to do. Not as easy to find people to do them with." — 28, New York.
  • Partiful is the only app anyone volunteered as useful. It organizes the invite, but not the occasion, so you end up talking to the people you came with.
  • Everyone described the same perfect day: a quiet morning, something active with friends, a shared meal, games at night. Alcohol is never the point.

The 5 core problems

  1. Finding the right people who want to do the same things.
  2. Finding new, fun things to do — easily.
  3. Starting conversations with people you don't know.
  4. Not trusting that events or apps have the right people.
  5. Cost and coordination — free, close, schedulable.

Where this leads →

People don't go out to drink, they go out to connect!
